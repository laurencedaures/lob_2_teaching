    Market making vs arbitrage

    Market makerArbitrageur
    What you doPost limit orders on both sides of one book and wait to be hit.Spot a price gap between two books and trade both at once.
    Role in the bookMaker: supplies liquidity.Taker: consumes liquidity on both legs.
    Where profit comes fromThe bid-ask spread, earned many times in small amounts.The gap itself: buy low on one exchange, sell high on the other.
    Main riskInventory risk: only one side fills and the price moves against your position.Leg risk: one leg fills, the other does not, or fills at a worse price.
    What it needsPatience, a sensible spread, control of inventory.Speed. The opportunity disappears as soon as others act on it.
    Effect on the marketNarrows spreads and deepens the book.Pulls prices on the two exchanges back together.

    Glossary

    Limit order book (LOB)
    The list of all resting limit orders on one exchange, sorted by price.
    Price-time priority
    The best price is served first. At the same price, the earliest order is served first.
    Spread
    The gap between the best ask and the best bid of one exchange.
    Maker / taker
    The maker posts a resting order. The taker crosses the book and trades against it.
    Realized P&L
    Profit from matched buys and sells: the spread you captured or the gap you locked in.
    Open-position P&L
    Gain or loss on the inventory you still hold, valued at the current mid price.
    Cross-venue arbitrage
    Exists when one exchange's best bid is higher than the other's best ask. Buying on one and selling on the other locks the difference.
    Leg risk
    Risk that the second trade of an arbitrage is filled late or at a worse price, so the profit shrinks or turns into a loss.
